Climate change refers to significant, long-term changes in the global climate. 
The Earth's climate has changed throughout history, but the current phase of warming is occurring much faster 
than in the past and is primarily driven by human activities. 
The burning of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas releases greenhouse gases 
like carbon dioxide (CO2) and methane (CH4) into the atmosphere. 
These gases trap heat, causing global temperatures to rise — a phenomenon known as the greenhouse effect.

The consequences of climate change are widespread and severe. 
Rising global temperatures lead to melting ice caps and glaciers, contributing to rising sea levels, 
which threaten coastal communities with flooding and erosion. 
Additionally, more frequent and intense extreme weather events such as hurricanes, droughts, and heatwaves have been observed. 
These changes disrupt ecosystems, agriculture, and water supplies, posing risks to biodiversity and human health.

Efforts to mitigate climate change focus on reducing greenhouse gas emissions and transitioning to renewable energy sources 
like solar, wind, and hydroelectric power. 
International agreements such as the Paris Agreement aim to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius 
above pre-industrial levels. 
Adaptation strategies, including building resilient infrastructure and protecting natural habitats, 
are also crucial to cope with the changes already underway.

Public awareness and individual actions, such as reducing energy consumption, supporting sustainable products, 
and advocating for policy change, play an essential role in addressing climate change. 
Scientific research continues to improve understanding of climate processes and inform effective solutions. 
Without urgent and coordinated global action, the impacts of climate change are expected to intensify, 
threatening future generations and the planet's health.
